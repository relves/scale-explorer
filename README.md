# Scale Explorer

An interactive, single-page tool for exploring major and minor keys: their scales, diatonic chords, and the secondary dominants that lead into them.

**▶ Try it live: [relves.github.io/scale-explorer](https://relves.github.io/scale-explorer/)**

## Features

- **Circle of fifths.** Major keys sit on the outer ring and their relative minors on the inner ring. Click any segment to change key. The selected key, its neighbours, and its relative major or minor are highlighted, and the hub shows the key signature.
- **Scale view.** The notes of the current key are listed with Roman numerals and correct enharmonic spelling (F♯ major uses sharps, E♭ minor uses flats).
- **Chord columns.** Click a scale degree to see the triads built on its root, with each chord tone labelled by interval (1, ♭3, 3, ♭5, 5, ♭7, 7). Chords that belong to the key are highlighted.
- **7th chords.** Switch from triads to maj7, 7, m7 and m7♭5 chords. The numerals change to match (Imaj7, ii7, V7, viiø7 …).
- **Highlight chord root's major scale.** Shows the chord root's major scale under each chord column, so you can see how each chord relates to it.
- **+ Dominant.** Adds the dominant 7th (V7, or a secondary dominant like V7/ii) that resolves into the selected chord. You can stack dominants into a chain of resolutions.
- **Explanations.** Each dominant comes with a short note on why it pulls into its target: the leading tone rising a half step, the ♭7 falling to the target's 3rd, and any notes that briefly step outside the key.

## How to use it

1. Pick a key on the circle of fifths.
2. Click a note in the scale to see the chords built on it.
3. Turn on **7th chords** and **+ Dominant** to see how each chord is approached.
4. Read the explanation panel under the wheel for the theory behind what's shown.
